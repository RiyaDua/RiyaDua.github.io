About Me

Hi, I’m Riya, a recent graduate with a Master’s in Bioinformatics from Johns Hopkins University. My journey into this field began during my undergrad in biotechnology, when a course on computational biology first sparked my curiosity. That interest quickly turned into passion during my first project on cervical cancer, where I identified hub genes critical to disease progression. That research later became my first-author publication in PLoS ONE, and the push I needed to pursue bioinformatics more seriously.

What excites me most about this field is how diverse the data can be, from raw sequences to clinical records, and how many ways there are to extract meaningful biological insight from it. I love exploring different types of datasets and asking, “What can this data tell us that we don’t already know?”

In my work today, I focus on NGS data analysis, machine learning, and clinical bioinformatics. I'm especially drawn to projects in precision medicine, where research has a direct impact on improving patient outcomes. I'm always open to collaborating, learning from others, and contributing to work that bridges data and biology in meaningful ways.
